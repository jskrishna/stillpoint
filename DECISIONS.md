# What is waiting on a decision

Everything in this file is blocked on somebody choosing something, not on
somebody writing code. Each one is also written down where the code waits for
it — this page exists so the list can be read in one go by the person who has
to decide, rather than found by reading five files.

Nothing here is guessed at in the meantime. A `null` is an honest gap; a
placeholder that looks like a product decision becomes one.

The "then" column is an estimate of the work after an answer arrives, and
estimates are what they are.

| **Sign-off on the risk screen**                                 | a licensed clinician   | A phrase screen, tuned for recall, covering English, Hinglish and Hindi thinly and saying so. | **What "use my anonymous sessions to improve the app" means** | a lawyer, then product | The optional consent item is validated, stored, and **read by nothing**: no analytics, no export for training, no aggregate over session text beyond the user's own insights. It fails safe — declining and accepting are treated the same, because the answer is never consulted — but "improve the app" is a scope rather than a purpose, and whatever first reads that column is what the people who ticked it will have agreed to. Law 25 wants a specified purpose. Either narrow the wording to what will actually be done, or drop the item until something needs it. | `packages/protocol/src/onboarding.ts`, `App\Domain\ConsentItem`, `users.accepted_consent` | a lawyer's answer, then ~1 hour |
| **Which privacy law the consent screen is written for** | a lawyer | Nothing in the product's own copy names a law — the consent items say what the product does in plain words, which is the right way round. What changed is that Canada is the first market, so PIPEDA and Québec's Law 25 apply rather than India's DPDP, and Law 25 is the stricter on consent. The code comments name them so whoever asks knows who to ask; **nobody here has read any of them.** This matters before a listener is bound and before the marketing site makes a privacy claim. | `apps/web/src/lib/voice/user-ear.ts`, the consent screens | a lawyer's time |

| **Taking money at all** | product | **No billing, but the plans are reachable now.** There is still no payment provider, no checkout and no subscription: nobody can _buy_ anything. What exists is a **grant** — `PATCH /admin/users/{id}/plan` in the console, guarded the way role changes are (nobody sets their own) and trailed in `plan_changes` — so an admin can put a pilot account or a coach on Plus by hand, and the prices in `plans.ts` are for plans somebody can at least be on. `Plan` has always enforced what each one allows, and a test now pins that a granted plan is what the allowance honours. What is missing is the provider, the checkout and the webhook, which would write to the same trail with `changed_by` null. The grant is not a stopgap that has to be removed first: comping an account is a thing a product needs anyway. | `plans.ts`, `App\Domain\Plan`, `AdminUserController::updatePlan()` | ~1 week, and a provider account |

| **Whether an export includes a crisis disclosure** | safeguarding | **It does not.** "Export everything" is the account plus the whole journal, and a safety-stopped session is never journalled — so the words from the turn that stopped it live in `safety_flags.excerpt` and nowhere the person can reach. The copy under the button says "Every session you have finished", which a stopped one is not, so the sentence is accurate while the label over-promises. A subject access request under PIPEDA or DPDP plainly covers it and it is their own sentence; handing somebody their own crisis disclosure in a file, unprompted, months later, is not obviously a kindness. Needs the same person as the erasure question below it, not a refactor. | `Settings.tsx`'s `exportData`, `safety_flags.excerpt` | a decision, then ~half a day |
| **Whether "Never share" should unshare the past** | product | **Nothing.** Choosing "Never share" stops new sessions being shared and refuses turning sharing on, and it leaves entries already shared exactly as they are — the precedent being that ending a pairing does not unshare them either, because `shared_with_coach` is a decision the user made about one session. So somebody who panics and chooses "Never share" still has to unshare entry by entry, and the quickest way to stop everything is `/me/coaches`, which ends the pairing. Whether the setting should offer "and unshare the 12 sessions already shared" is a product question about what that phrase promises; the answer changes a screen, not a rule. | `App\Domain\CoachSharing`, `packages/protocol/src/coach.ts` | ~half a day |

## Deployment, before real people

These are in `deploy/README.md` at more length. They are infrastructure
decisions rather than product ones.

- **Where the database lives, and who backs it up.** Encrypted columns cannot
  be reconstructed from anywhere else, and `APP_KEY` cannot be recovered.
- **Where TLS terminates.** `deploy/nginx.conf` listens on port 80 and assumes
  something in front holds the certificate. Bearer tokens over plain HTTP are
  bearer tokens in public.
- **Cookie mode instead of a bearer token in `localStorage`.** The weakness is
  documented at the top of `apps/web/src/lib/api.ts`; a Content-Security-Policy
  currently stops a stolen token being sent anywhere, which is a mitigation and
  not the fix. Adopting it is a change to how every surface authenticates, and
  it needs Sanctum's `sanctum/csrf-cookie` route turned back on in the same
  change.

## And one that needs a phone, not a decision

**The mobile app has never run on a device.** There is no simulator in the
development container and no device attached to CI. `e2e/mobile.mjs` drives the
web export through the whole journey in a real browser, which covers the
screens, the reducer, the API binding and the navigation — and cannot cover the
keychain, text-to-speech, `tel:` links, the share sheet, the splash screen,
safe-area insets on a notched device, or being backgrounded mid-session. Treat
the first run on hardware as a test pass that has not happened.
